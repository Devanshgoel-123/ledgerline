# Video 1 — What Ledgerline is

Length: about 2 minutes 20 seconds.
This is the only tour. Later videos stay on one new feature.

Follow `00-shoot-rules.md`.

Start signed in on Overview. Film the login screen as a separate 8-second plate with nobody clicking **Continue with Google**.

## 0:00 — Title plate

Open http://localhost:3000 in a signed-out window, or use a still of the login screen.

Hold the headline: "See where every rupee actually went."

Voice:

> UPI apps tell you a payment went through. They do not tell you what the month felt like. Ledgerline reads the bank alerts you already get, and turns them into the month. Those alerts have to be turned on in the bank app. If the mail is off, there is nothing to read.

Do not click sign-in.

## 0:12 — The month

Cut to the signed-in app. Sidebar visible. Overview.

Point at the month title, then the line under it: spent, invested, received, transaction count.

Voice:

> This is one month. Spent is money that left as expenditure, after refunds. Received sits on its own, so money coming in does not look like you spent less. Invested is kept out of that spent number. Ledgerline is not a bank, and it does not hold a password.

Hold 2 seconds.

## 0:32 — The shape of the days

Scroll until the daily chart is the whole frame. Hover one tall bar. Do not read a merchant's UPI id.

Voice:

> Each bar is a day, from the 1st. The line is the daily cap from Settings. A tall day is the one to look at.

Scroll to the **Rhythm** heading and the heatmap. Stop.

Voice:

> Darker days are heavier. The sentence above the map says how many days ran past the cap, and whether weekends run hotter than weekdays.

## 0:52 — Where it went

Click **Lifestyle** in the sidebar. Hold the category breakdown.

Voice:

> Categories are the point. Food, travel, household, outing. The labels come from the narration, and from a correction you make once.

Click **Apps**. Hold one vendor card that shows a logo. Do not open a UPI id list if it fills the screen with ids.

Voice:

> Shops are apps. Name, what you spent, the category. Change the category here and later payments follow it. A lot of Indian retail and D2C names already resolve to a logo.

Click **People**. Hold the Friends or Family group.

Voice:

> People are the payments that are not a shop. A friend, family, office. Mark them once. They stay people, across months.

## 1:22 — The ledger

Click **Transactions**. Hold the top of the list: date, merchant, where it went, amount. Do not read the UPI column.

Voice:

> Every row is one bank line. If the guess is wrong, you correct the row. You do not re-import the month to fix a label.

Scroll inside the table for one second so the list moves and the page chrome stays. Then stop.

## 1:38 — Where the rows come from

Click **Import**. Hold the **Bank mail** panel, including the line: "In your bank app, turn on email alerts for transactions. If those mails are off, a scan has nothing to import." Do not click **Scan bank mail**. Do not click **Clear imported data**.

Voice:

> Two ways in. Bank mail, read-only, and only the senders for the banks you pick. Or a statement PDF. In the bank app, email alerts for transactions have to be on. If those mails are off, a scan has nothing to import. The Google password stays with Google. A PDF password opens that file and is not saved.

Click **Statement match**. Hold the heading **Approve vendor UPI ids**. Do not upload a file.

Voice:

> Mail says what the alert said. The statement says what the bank posted. This screen is where a vendor's UPI id gets confirmed. It does not insert new transactions by itself.

## 2:00 — Close

Click **Settings**. Point at the daily limit. Leave Telegram and Gmail as they are. Do not open delete account.

Voice:

> The limit, Telegram, and Gmail live here. The loop is simple. Bank mail or a statement comes in, the ledger labels it, and the month shows the shape of the spending. You correct a vendor once. You do not keep a spreadsheet.

Click **Overview**. Hold the month title until the clip ends.
